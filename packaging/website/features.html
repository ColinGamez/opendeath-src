Everything the deck does.

OpenDeath is one desktop app with two jobs: give you a serious workspace, and keep the route healthy while you use it. Here is the whole surface.

02 — Recovery

Keep the
session alive.

The recovery layer watches for a free limit, changes the exit, and resumes the chats that are waiting. It does this without closing the workspace.

Detect

The event stream reports a provider free limit for a live chat.

Rotate

OpenDeath probes candidate exits and avoids IPs it has already burned.

Verify

The new route must answer before anything is sent to the provider.

Continue

Every waiting chat receives continue in parallel, with duplicate protection.

01

Relaunch resume

Waiting chats are stored in pending.json. On the next launch they are restored and resumed once the route is ready.

02

Quiet window

A short per-chat delay plus a rotation lock prevents the same limit from being answered twice.

03

Explicit status

The deck reports what happened, how many chats were updated, and what is left for you to send.

03 — Workspace

A full room,
not a chat box.

Sessions, tabs, diffs, a terminal, a browser, and dictation. The network deck sits under the title bar where it belongs.

01

Sessions and tabs

Keep several threads open, switch without losing scroll position, and reopen a closed tab from the strip.

02

Diffs and terminal

Review file changes beside the conversation and run commands in a real terminal pane.

03

Built-in browser

Open a page, inspect it, and bring what you find back into the session without a second app.

04

Voice dictation

Speak a prompt when typing is slower. Dictation is available anywhere the composer is.

05

Themes and color

Dark by default, with light and system themes, custom backgrounds, rainbow accents, and a text color that stays readable.

06

A real control room

Basic, Advanced, and Personalization tabs replace the wall of settings with three clear groups.

04 — Distribution

Installed
like an app.

A per-user setup wizard, a single portable executable, or one command on Linux. All run the same build and keep their data in the same place.

01

Setup wizard

Checks for OpenCode Desktop, offers official VPN packages, creates shortcuts, and registers an uninstaller.

02

Portable build

A single executable that extracts to a temporary folder. No shortcuts, no registry entry, no installer.

03

Bundled engine

The OpenCode engine ships inside the app. You do not need a separate CLI install.

04

One dependency

The engine ships inside the app. The WARP client is the only thing that has to be installed, and the wizard puts it in place first.

05

Pointed at itself

Desktop and Start menu shortcuts launch opendeath.exe, never the OpenCode window.

06

Clean uninstall

The uninstaller removes the app and shortcuts but keeps your settings and waiting chats.

07

Linux packages

AppImage, deb and rpm, plus a one-command installer that reads your distribution and verifies every checksum. Arch and CachyOS use the AUR package.

08

Terminal CLI

The same engine as an opendeath command. Tick the box in the Windows setup, or get it on PATH from the Linux installer.

05 — Boundaries

What it
doesn't do.

A shorter list, and an important one. OpenDeath is deliberately not an account and not a cloud.

  • No telemetry. No analytics, usage reporting, or crash upload endpoint was added.
  • No OpenDeath account. There is no OpenDeath server to sign in to.
  • No silent updates. A new build is downloaded in the background, but it only installs when you say yes to the restart.
  • No replacement for OpenCode Desktop. It installs alongside and keeps its own executable and data folder.
  • No hidden proxy. Provider traffic goes to the provider you configured, through the route you selected.
